Accessible Concert Venues

Accessibility details decide whether every fan can attend a show, and they belong in your advance sheet. This page highlights real venues that publish clear accessibility information, followed by our sample profiles, so you can see exactly what to ask any room before you book it.

Quick answer: Start with venues that publish specific, detailed accessibility pages. The Englert Theatre in Iowa City lists the exact rows and seat numbers for its wheelchair and transfer seating, and the Auditorium Theatre in Chicago takes requests for ASL interpretation, captioning and audio description when you buy tickets.

What we look for

Whether a venue is real or one of our sample profiles, these are the eight items our editors check. The more of them a venue clearly explains, the easier it is to advance a show there.

  • Step-free route from the street to the main floor, the bar and the restrooms.
  • A dedicated accessible viewing area with a clear view of the stage when the floor is full.
  • At least one accessible, all-gender restroom on the same level as the viewing area.
  • Assistive listening devices available at the box office without advance request.
  • A written policy for providing ASL interpretation, including who pays for it.
  • A quiet room or sensory kit with earplugs and fidget tools.
  • Accessible parking or a marked drop-off zone within one block of the entrance.
  • Free companion tickets for guests who need an assistant to attend.

These summaries come from each venue's own public accessibility information. Codex Venue has not visited or scored these venues and is not affiliated with them.

Check before you book: Venue policies, seating and contacts change. The details below were summarized from each venue's website in October 2026. Always confirm with the venue directly before making plans or putting details in an advance sheet.
Iowa City, IA

The Englert Theatre

221 E Washington Street · Historic theater with about 725 seats

Entrance
The main entrance is at street level on Washington Street, through two wide double doors and one single door that open outward.
Wheelchair and accessible seats
Main floor, row U, seats 1 through 10.
Transfer seats
Main floor seats E19–22 and F19–22, plus balcony seats C101–102 and C111–112.
Getting around inside
A house wheelchair is available from the house managers for moving through the building, and volunteer ushers staff the entrances at the venue's own events. The balcony and lounge are on the second floor.
Transit
Iowa City Transit routes 1, 2 and 9 stop at Washington and Linn, about 100 feet from the door.
Ticketing
Sold through the venue's AudienceView box office.
Contact
(319) 688-2653 or info@englert.org

Chicago, IL

Auditorium Theatre

50 E Ida B. Wells Drive · Landmark theater in the Auditorium Building

Entrance
The glass main doors involve three steps up to the lobby. A separate entrance offers ramp access with no steps; ask staff which door to use.
Seating
Nearly every seat involves steps in the lobby or the house, so ask Ticket Services about step-free seating before you buy.
ASL, captioning and audio description
Request at the time of ticket purchase.
Assistive listening
Free infrared listening devices from the coat check, first come, first served, with a photo ID held as a deposit.
Contact
Ticket Services Center, 312-341-2300

Tips for buyers and promoters

Put accessibility in your advance sheet, not just in your marketing. Ask the venue who receives accessibility requests on show day, list that person's name on the day sheet, and make sure your ticketing page explains how to request companion tickets, interpreters and captioning. The real venues above show how much easier booking becomes when a venue publishes seat locations and request policies in advance. When an artist's audience includes fans who need accommodations, these details can decide whether people buy at all.
