Accessible Concert Venues

These four Midwest rooms scored highest on our accessibility checklist this season. Each listing also includes capacity, ticketing platform and the talent buyer to contact, so you can shortlist and reach out in one pass.

Quick answer: Riverbend Hall in Des Moines is the most accessible room we list, with step-free entry on every level, a raised wheelchair platform with sightlines over a standing crowd, and an interpreter booked automatically for any show that requests one 14 days out.

How we rate accessibility

Our editors visit each venue in person and check eight items. A venue has to meet at least six of them to appear on this page.

  • Step-free route from the street to the main floor, the bar and the restrooms.
  • A dedicated accessible viewing area with a clear view of the stage when the floor is full.
  • At least one accessible, all-gender restroom on the same level as the viewing area.
  • Assistive listening devices available at the box office without advance request.
  • A written policy for providing ASL interpretation, including who pays for it.
  • A quiet room or sensory kit with earplugs and fidget tools.
  • Accessible parking or a marked drop-off zone within one block of the entrance.
  • Free companion tickets for guests who need an assistant to attend.

Tips for buyers and promoters

Put accessibility in your advance sheet, not just in your marketing. Ask the venue who receives accessibility requests on show day, list that person's name on the day sheet, and make sure your ticketing page explains how to request companion tickets. When an artist's audience skews toward fans who need accommodations, these details can decide whether people buy at all.
