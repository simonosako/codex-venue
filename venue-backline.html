Venue Backline: What Venues Provide and What to Bring

Backline is the stage gear a band plays through: drums, amplifiers, keyboards and the stands and cables that go with them. Every venue profile on Codex Venue lists the house backline, but what a room provides depends heavily on its size. This guide explains what to expect and how to ask for what you need.

Quick answer: Most venues provide a basic drum kit without cymbals, at least one bass amp, a mixing console, monitors and microphones. Guitars, cymbals, pedals, synths and anything personal to the artist's sound are almost always the band's responsibility to carry or rent.

Backline versus production

Backline

What the band plays through

Drum kits, bass and guitar amplifiers, keyboards, stands, drum risers and rugs. Shared backline lets several acts play one bill without swapping heavy gear between sets.

Production

What the audience hears and sees

Mixing consoles, the main speaker system, monitors, microphones, DI boxes, stage inputs and lighting. The venue's production manager oversees this, and it is usually run by house engineers.

What the band almost always brings

  • Cymbals, snare drum, kick pedal and any electronic drum pads.
  • Guitars and basses, pedalboards, instrument cables and spare strings.
  • Synths, samplers, playback rigs and a backup laptop with the show files.
  • In-ear monitor packs and any wireless microphones beyond what the house provides.
  • Gaff tape, labeled cables, spare batteries and a printed stage plot for local crew.

How to request backline in your advance

1. Send a stage plot

A simple drawing showing where each player stands, where amps and the drum riser go, and where power is needed. Local crew use it to set the stage before you arrive.

2. Send an input list

A numbered list of every microphone and direct line you need, in channel order. It tells the house engineer whether the room has enough inputs for your show.

3. Name exact models only when they matter

Ask for "a 4x10 bass cabinet" rather than a specific brand unless the sound truly depends on it. Flexible requests are easier for venues and rental shops to fill.

4. Confirm what is shared

On a multi-act bill, agree in writing which acts use the house kit and amps, and who supplies cymbals and snare for the openers.

Send the stage plot, input list and backline request together, ideally three weeks before show day. Rental shops get booked up during festival season, so late requests can mean paying more for whatever is left.

Drum risers and touring gear take up stage and floor space, which can lower how many tickets a room can sell. Our venue capacity guide explains how to account for that. If you are booking a room for an audience that needs accessible viewing, check our accessible concert venues list too.

Gear changes often: House backline breaks, gets replaced and gets loaned out. Treat any listed gear as a starting point and confirm it with the venue's production manager during your advance.

Backline questions to put in your advance

  • What house backline is available for this date, and is any of it reserved for another act?
  • Are cymbals, a snare or breakables included, and is there a fee for using them?
  • How many stage inputs and monitor mixes are available?
  • Can we bring our own console or in-ear system, and how much notice do you need?
  • Which local rental shop do you recommend, and can it deliver to the loading door?
  • Who should we call on show day if a piece of house gear fails?
