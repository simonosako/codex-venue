What every venue profile covers

Rooms and capacity

Licensed capacity for each room, standing and seated layouts, and the number we recommend you sell to so the floor still feels good.

Accessibility

Entrances, restrooms, viewing areas, assistive listening, interpreter policy, sensory accommodations and companion ticket rules.

Ticketing

Which platform the venue sells through, how fees are split, settlement timing, and whether outside ticketing is allowed for private rentals.

Talent buyer contact

The person who places holds and makes offers, their preferred way to be reached, and how far out they typically book.

Load in and load out

Dock or curb access, stairs and elevators, earliest load-in time, curfew, parking for buses and trailers, and stagehand requirements.

Backline and production

House gear, console models, monitor setup, input count, and the local rental shops tours rely on when something fails.

Merch

Venue cut on soft goods and on music, whether the venue sells or the band sells, table location and card reader policy.

Hospitality

Green room size, showers, Wi-Fi, catering or buyout amounts, and how many guest list spots come standard.

Who uses Codex Venue

Booking agents routing a tour use our capacity filters to find the right size room in each market. Talent buyers use profiles to check another venue's terms before they counter an offer. Tour managers and production managers print the load-in and backline sections the night before a show. Festival and nonprofit organizers use the accessibility data to choose venues where every guest can attend comfortably.

About us

Codex Venue was started in 2024 by Marisol Okafor, a former tour manager who spent eight years advancing club tours across the Midwest, and Theo Brandt, a talent buyer who ran a 600-capacity room in Omaha. They were tired of advance sheets that were half guesswork. Today a small team of four editors updates venue profiles every season and confirms each change directly with venue staff.
